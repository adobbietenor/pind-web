# M3.4 review findings: 1-on-1 (A28, A29)

An independent adversarial review, 29 Oct 2026, run against pind-staging from branch
`phase3/issue-log`. It covers the brief (`docs/m3.4-review-brief.md`) and the 16 Oct changes the
brief does not mention: reports on people (A10), `my_chats` (A19) and the thread purge (Q11).
Nothing was fixed.

**Reproductions:** `tests/policies/review-m3.4.test.ts`. It is not in `test:policies`. Run it with:

    node --env-file=.dev.vars --test --test-concurrency=1 tests/policies/review-m3.4.test.ts

The last run had 12 cases: 6 fail (the findings below) and 6 pass (the "holds" cases). A failing
case fails today and will pass once the fault is fixed. Every leak was reproduced as a signed-in
person using their own token. The service key only set things up and cleaned up:
- it created the people and gatherings;
- it moved a gathering's start into the past and ran `admin_groups_tick`, which is what pg_cron
  does every 10 minutes;
- it read the delivery queue for R5, which is a robustness finding, not a leak.

The brake was never pulled, because it is global and another reviewer shares staging. The run
cleans up after itself and asserts that it left no rows behind.

## Findings

### R1 — LEAK: someone who isn't women-only eligible can choose "women only" and learn other people's gender

- **Rule broken:** V14 and V5. visibility.md D1 says gender "must never be readable per person".
  V3 releases gender only as counts, and only at 5 or more people.
- **Severity:** leak (per-person gender, and a nonbinary person's women-only flag).
- **Reproduced:** yes, deterministically. The failing cases are
  `R1 LEAK: a man who picks 'women only'…` and `R1b LEAK: the same for a nonbinary person who did NOT opt into women-only`.
- **Reproduction:**
  1. Cal (a man) and five others turn 1-on-1 on with "everyone". Cal's `solo_people` lists
     Ann, Bea, Dov, Eve and Fox.
  2. Cal updates his own pin to `solo_visibility = 'women_only'`. The database accepts it, and
     the app offers the choice to everyone (`SoloSection.tsx`).
  3. His list is now Ann, Bea and Eve. From the difference he learns three things: Ann and Bea
     are women, Eve is a nonbinary person who opted into women-only, and Dov and Fox are not
     eligible.
  4. Fox (nonbinary, not opted in) can do the same thing.
- **Cause:**
  - `can_see_solo` has the clause `mine = 'everyone' or is_women_only_eligible(p_target)`. It
    filters by the target's eligibility for any chooser.
  - Nothing refuses `women_only` to someone who isn't eligible. Squads do refuse it ("chosen…
    only by an eligible person", M3.7), and the women-only room places only eligible people.
- **Caveat:** gender is self-declared (`people_private` is owner-writable). A man who declares
  himself a woman gets the eligible person's view. That is the existing V5 trust model and was
  not reviewed here. Eligible people learn each other's eligibility from "women only" by design,
  as they do in the women-only room.
- **Would the harness catch it?** No. P211 only tests eligible choosers (Wyn) and ineligible
  viewers. It never tests an ineligible chooser.

### R2 — INFERENCE: after the start, a sender can tell a declined proposal from an unanswered one

- **Rule broken:**
  - The lifecycle decision says a declined proposal is "indistinguishable from an unanswered
    one, in every respect".
  - `my_solo_export`'s own comment says "A decline is never told to the sender, here either".
    It maps both to `not accepted` after the start precisely to keep this.
  - P225 asserts "After the start, a declined and an unanswered proposal read the same to their
    sender".
  - The decision's text guarantees this only "until the start". The export and P225 show the
    intent also covers after the start. If Alex reads the decision literally, this drops to a
    documented gap.
- **Severity:** inference (of a decline).
- **Reproduced:** yes, deterministically, once the 10-minute job has run after the start. There
  are two independent oracles, and the sender drives both alone:
  - **R2a, the export** (`R2a INFERENCE: … the sender switching their own 1-on-1 off…`):
    1. Cal proposes to Ann with line 1 and to Bea with line 2. Ann declines and Bea never
       answers.
    2. After the start and the tick, Cal's export reads `not accepted` / `not accepted`, which
       is correct.
    3. Cal then switches his own 1-on-1 off. Pins stay editable until the effective end.
    4. His export now reads **`closed`** for Ann's proposal and **`not accepted`** for Bea's.
    5. Removing his pin does the same.
  - **R2b, withdrawing** (`R2b INFERENCE: … withdraw_solo succeeds on a declined proposal…`):
    1. Dov kept the proposal ids that `my_solo_proposals` gave him before the start.
    2. After the start, `withdraw_solo` **succeeds** on the one Ann declined.
    3. It is **refused** on the unanswered one ("refused: no such proposal").
- **Cause:**
  - `admin_groups_tick` expires only `status = 'open'`, so a declined proposal stays `declined`.
  - "Declined" is still "pending" to `solo_pending`. Several things still act on pending
    proposals after the start:
    - `pins_solo_closes_proposals` (switching off, the women-only switch, removing a pin before
      the effective end);
    - `blocks_blocker_leaves_group`, which closes at every gathering, past ones included;
    - the tick's withdrawn-gathering close;
    - `withdraw_solo`.
  - The same mechanism also fires on the recipient's acts after the start. If Ann blocks Cal, or
    the gathering is withdrawn, Cal's export shows `closed` for the declined proposal only.
    Those paths were not run separately.
- **Fix direction (for Alex):** at the start, expire declined proposals too, and keep the
  recipient's answer somewhere the sender can't read (the metrics count `declined`). The
  alternative is that every closer, and `withdraw_solo`, ignore proposals once the gathering has
  started.
- **Would the harness catch it?** No.
  - P218 checks before the start.
  - P225 checks the export after the start but before any later action.
  - Nothing withdraws or closes a proposal after the start.

### R5 — ROBUSTNESS: a queued #8 is still delivered after its proposal closes, even across a block

- **Rule broken:** V4 (blocks). The recipient must not "see one across a block, from someone now
  out of 1-on-1". V22 applies to notifications. The brake's own reasoning (decisions: a #8 must
  be dropped, not held) is applied to the brake only.
- **Severity:** robustness. The text reaching someone across a block is leak-shaped.
- **Reproduced:** yes, observed in the delivery queue (`R5 ROBUSTNESS: the recipient blocks the sender seconds after the proposal…`).
  1. Gus proposes to Hal, and Hal blocks Gus straight away.
  2. The proposal closes, but the #8 row "Gus asked to meet you 1-on-1 at …" stays in
     `admin_pending_notifications`.
  3. The every-minute delivery job would push or email it to Hal.
  4. Harness people have no token or email, so nothing actually went out. This is why the case
     reads the queue rather than an inbox, and why it is classed as robustness.
- **Cause:**
  - `notify_solo_proposal` writes the #8 when the proposal is inserted, and the row carries no
    link to the proposal.
  - Nothing that closes a proposal touches it. Those are: a block, a withdraw, a report or hide
    of the sender, the recipient's women-only switch, and an accept elsewhere.
  - The delivery query only excludes #8 rows while the brake is pulled.
  - A failed send is retried up to 3 times, a minute apart, which widens the window.
- **Same mechanism, not separately run:**
  - a sender hidden by H9 within the minute still has their name pushed;
  - a sender who withdraws within the minute still pushes "asked to meet you", and the
    recipient then finds nothing there.
- **Would the harness catch it?** No. P227 covers the brake's drop only.

### R6 — LEAK (race): a block racing an accept leaves a 1-on-1 plan standing across the block

- **Rule broken:**
  - V4: no block, either way.
  - Decisions, M3.4 lifecycle: "blocking the other leaves".
  - The brief asks: "can a plan be made across a block … by racing … a block against an
    accept".
- **Severity:** leak.
  - The person who was blocked keeps a confirmed spot and time with the person who blocked them.
  - Both keep the thread and "I'm here".
  - The blocked person's `my_chats` names the blocker as their partner.
  - The blocker was told "Hal said yes: you're meeting at …".
  - Instagram handles are still protected, because `can_see_handle` checks blocks itself.
- **Reproduced:** yes, from two ordinary sessions. The case is `R6 LEAK (race): the sender blocks while the recipient accepts…`.
  1. Each trial is a fresh gathering. Gus proposes to Hal.
  2. Hal calls `respond_to_solo(accept)` while Gus inserts a block on Hal, both fired together.
     A few trials stagger them by 0–40 ms.
  3. It reproduced in both runs, within the first 10 trials. In the reported run, trial 9
     (0 ms) left the plan in `spot_set` with both seats active and the block in place. Hal read
     both members through his own session, and Gus read the plan.
  4. Because it is a race, a quiet run could miss it. If no trial reproduces it, the case
     returns without failing and logs that it did not.
- **Cause, in the SQL** (READ COMMITTED):
  1. `respond_to_solo` locks the proposal row (`FOR UPDATE`).
  2. Its `can_see_solo` check runs before the block has committed, so it passes.
  3. Meanwhile the block's AFTER trigger, `blocks_blocker_leaves_group`, runs its `crew_members`
     UPDATE first. That statement's snapshot is taken before the accept commits, so it finds no
     plan to leave.
  4. The trigger only then calls `close_solo_proposals`, which waits on the proposal lock.
  5. When it resumes, the proposal is `accepted`, which is not pending, so nothing happens.
  6. Nothing re-checks later.
- **Hide racing an accept holds** (by reasoning). In `people_solo_follows_hide` the dissolve loop
  is a later statement and gets a fresh snapshot after the lock wait.
- **Fix direction:** make the block trigger close proposals first (waiting on the lock) and then
  leave plans, in a later statement that sees the committed plan. Or take a per-pair advisory
  lock in both `respond_to_solo` and the block trigger. Either way, add a case that races them.
- **Would the harness catch it?** No. P212, P220 and P221 apply blocks one after another.

## Observations: noted, no failing case

- **Someone out of 1-on-1 can see it happen in their own export.**
  - A report or hide closes every pending proposal, sent and received, at one instant.
    `my_solo_export` shows them all as `closed`, with identical `answered_at` on the received
    side.
  - This matches the design (they see an empty section), but the export makes it sharp.
  - The rule says they cannot "learn that they are out".
- **The recipient's export is finer than their list.** It tells `withdrawn` from `closed`, and
  timestamps each close. That is low value, but every `closed` there corresponds to one of
  the reasons the list deliberately merges.
- **Under the brake, the recipient's `my_solo_export` still lists open received proposals**
  (spot, time and line, without a name). The rule is that, while the brake is pulled, nobody can
  "see a received proposal". Whether the export counts is a call for Alex (export means
  "everything we hold"). `respond_to_solo(decline)` also still works by id under the brake.
  These were reasoned from the SQL, not run, because the brake was not pulled.
- **A change of gender or `include_in_women_only` doesn't close pending proposals**, unlike the
  women-only choice. The sender keeps "Sent", and the recipient's list hides it until it changes
  back. Robustness, not a leak.
- **A third person sees two people leave their list together** and can guess they have a plan.
  This is Alex's approved call ("Narrowed by the plan").
- **Brake timing (can't be tested from a client).** The delivery job reads the pending list, then
  sends. A #8 read just before a pull is still sent, within about a minute. `admin_set_solo_brake`
  can't reach a request the Worker already holds.

## Attacked and found holding

Cases that pass, with their names:

- **The decline before the start** (`R2-0 HOLDS`). The sender's list, export, re-send refusal
  ("already asked", same code and words) and withdraw read the same for a declined and an
  unanswered proposal.
- **Reason inference** (`R3 HOLDS`). Someone left a list for one of five reasons: a block, a
  report (`hidden_from_solo`), women only, switching off, or a plan with someone else. A
  proposal to each is refused with an identical code, message, detail and hint ("refused: not
  available for 1-on-1").
- **The plan from outside** (`R4a HOLDS`). A third person at the gathering can't do any of these:
  - `join_squad` the plan (refused; `is_squad` is false for a 1-on-1);
  - report the plan as a crew (no insert policy matches a 1-on-1 crew, so the `report_takes_out_of_solo`
    crew branch is dead code);
  - read the `crews` or `crew_members` rows;
  - see it in `my_chats`;
  - end it with `leave_group`.
- **The sealed seen time (A19)** (`R4b HOLDS`).
  - `crew_members.last_seen_at` can't be selected, filtered on, ordered by, or embedded from
    `crews`.
  - `crew_seen` marks only the caller's own row.
  - `my_chats` is SECURITY INVOKER, and its definer helpers answer only for the caller.
- **Reports on people (A10) inside a plan** (`R4c HOLDS`).
  - A non-safety report from the 1-on-1 thread (`context_crew_id`) takes the partner out of
    1-on-1 and leaves the plan standing, which is below H9.
  - An outsider can't forge a report from that thread.
  - H9 counts distinct reporters per person, whichever door the report came through, so the
    thread path does not skip the count.
- **Visitors and people versus admin doors** (`R4d HOLDS`).
  - A visitor can't call any of: `solo_people`, `my_solo`, `my_solo_proposals`,
    `my_solo_export`, `propose_solo`, `respond_to_solo`, `withdraw_solo`, `my_chats`,
    `crew_seen`, `admin_delete_expired_crew_messages`, the brake, or "end every plan".
  - A signed-in person can't call the admin ones or `admin_solo_metrics`.

Reasoned from the SQL (no client test, or already covered by the main harness):

- **`after_tick` spreading ticks.** `after_shared` needs `in_crew` for both people on every crew
  it writes to, so a tick never lands on a plan the ticker isn't in (P224 also covers this).
- **Two accepts at once, and a third seat.** Both are held by `crew_members_one_active_per_kind`
  and `solo_two_seats` (P219).
- **H9 and one person.** A single safety report is H9 by definition, and below it the plan
  stands (P221). The person can always leave, which is by design.
- **A hide racing an accept** holds (see R6).
- **The platform switch is cosmetic.** No SQL reads `app_switches`. Only the app's `offered`
  flag uses it.
- **`admin_solo_metrics` is service-only.** At a small gathering its counts identify
  individuals, but only to the admin, who can already read the rows.
- **`pins.updated_at` isn't granted (Q37)**, so toggling 1-on-1 leaves no visible trace on the
  people list.
- **Realtime.** Only `room_messages` and `crew_messages` are published. No 1-on-1 table is.
- **The Q11 purge** is service-only (P254).
  - It deletes every thread message at the effective end + 24 h + 30 days.
  - A report that pointed at a message keeps its row. The FK sets it to null, and the report
    constraints are written "is null or …", so setting it null breaks nothing.
- **`app/src/lib/solo.ts`, `SoloSection.tsx`, `myevents.ts` and `my-events.tsx`** decide only
  what is drawn. Every gate is in the database, except the women-only choice, which neither side
  restricts (R1).
